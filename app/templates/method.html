Research approach

To identify regulated plant species comprehensively, we carried out systematic research using authoritative and reliable sources, in four steps.

  1. Data collection from authoritative sources

    We reviewed and extracted information from government websites (state and provincial departments of agriculture, environmental protection and natural resources), academic institutions (university extension programs specialising in invasive and noxious plants), and reputable non-governmental organisations focused on plant conservation and invasive species management.

  2. Region-specific lists

    For each state, province and country we compiled a list of regulated plant species, capturing variations in regulatory definitions (e.g. noxious, invasive) and the species classifications unique to each jurisdiction.

  3. Comparison with federal lists

    Regional lists were compared with federal noxious plant lists from the relevant agencies (USDA in the United States, federal regulations in Canada, national restrictions in Australia) to identify discrepancies, overlaps and gaps between regional and national regulation.

  4. Verification and cross-referencing

    Findings were cross-referenced for accuracy and consistency, including validating species names and classifications to account for synonyms and taxonomic updates.

The result is a consistent view of regulated plant species that shows where plant management policies align and diverge across jurisdictions.

Scope

The database is compiled from official regulatory lists and updated as regulations change. For each regulated plant it records:

  • Common and scientific names
  • Regulation status across jurisdictions
  • Distribution across regions
Definition

Speciesn. In a regulated plant list, a genus or species includes all of its species, subspecies, cultivars, varieties and forms, including genetically modified variants, unless specifically excluded. It excludes hybrids with another species unless otherwise stated.
